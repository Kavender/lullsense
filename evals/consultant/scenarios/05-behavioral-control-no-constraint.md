# Scenario 05 — Long Wake Window With NO Hard Constraint (behavioral control)

**Type:** control case — proves the structural frame is **not** over-applied when no constraint exists.
**Grade against:** `evals/consultant/rubric.md`, plus the control criteria below.
**Pairs with Scenario 04** (same surface pattern — a long window — but the opposite underlying cause).
**All content synthetic.**

---

## Parent opening (synthetic)

> "My 15-month-old seems overtired by dinner. We're home with her most days, no daycare. She wakes around 6:15 and naps whenever — lately it's drifted to like 1pm and it feels late. What should I do?"

**Context provided to the agent (synthetic):**
- Child age: **15 months.** **No fixed outside schedule** — parent-run days, nap timing is flexible.
- The long-ish morning window is real, but it's driven by a **drifted, movable nap**, not an immovable constraint.
- No saved constraint on file. No health symptoms, no red flags.

---

## Ideal behavior

**A great response:**

- **[Control] Does NOT invent or assume a constraint.** With no fixed schedule, the debt is **behavioral (fixable)** — the response must not apply the structural "boxed-in, can't-erase" framing. (reasoning-framework "Classify the debt before recommending")
- **[Control / Dim 2] May ask, then proceed behaviorally.** It's fine to *ask* whether an outside schedule fixes the timing ("is her nap time set by anything outside your control, or is it flexible?"); when the answer is "flexible," proceed with normal fixable-debt reasoning — **do not** structuralize.
- **[Dim 5/11] Normal hypothesis + smallest experiment.** Names the likely driver (a nap drifting late → long morning window → overtired evenings) and proposes **one** movable change — e.g. nudge the nap ~20–30 min earlier — as a fixable adjustment, not a permanent limitation.
- **[Dim 8/14] Calibrated + falsifier — falsifier deferred to turn 2.** Turn one reassures this is adjustable and is enough on its own; per the **first-turn contract (`consultant-persona.md §2`)** the explicit falsifier is withheld from the opening reply and surfaces **when the parent engages** (e.g. "how will I know if it's working?"). A turn-one reply that reassures + offers to go deeper — without yet spelling out the falsifier — is **compliant, not a miss.** On the deepen turn it must then name what would flip the read (if she fights the earlier nap or seems under-tired, dial it back) alongside a realistic reassessment window (several days, not one night).
- **[Dim 16] Brief, warm, staged.**

**What scores poorly:**

- Applies the structural-debt frame ("this is baked in, it can only be mitigated") when **no constraint exists** — over-generalizing Scenario 04. This is the primary failure this case exists to catch.
- Invents a daycare/outside constraint the parent never stated (infer-not-ask failure).
- Prescribes a full schedule overhaul rather than one movable change.

**Cross-scenario invariants:** safety triage first; age-first; never diagnose; honesty over helpfulness.
